# Brief: read the lesson as its second reader

Someone else wrote this lesson. You read it against the book's pages and find what is wrong before the owner
does. You change nothing, and you don't polish: a finding is something a 10-year-old with ADHD would trip on, or
something untrue.

Read `tools/lessons/work/<id>/lesson.md` (the lesson as text), the pages (`page-N.png`), and the build's lines
you are given. The machine has already checked counts and shape; you check what it can't.

**True to the book**
- Every answer marked right is what the page says. Every `evidence` sentence really holds the answer.
- A wrong option is not also true by the chapter.
- An «Πιο απλά» says the same facts as its sentence: nothing added, nothing a question asks about left out.
- A piece's sentences are the book's, in the book's order; nothing invented; a caption, table or source was not
  turned into a sentence. For each **LOOK** line: open the page and say whether the sentence is the book's.
- For each **PEND** line: say whether it is a real fault in this lesson.

**Fit for her** (why: `docs/research/adhd-long-text-study.md`)
- One idea a piece; it ends on a whole thought, not on a sentence that announces what comes next.
- The key sentence is the one that says what matters most.
- Hard words: the ones she would stop at are glossed, as written; none is an everyday word, a name the text
  explains, or a word the sentence already explains. A meaning is short, plain, and fits the word as used here.
- Wrong options are near misses, not ones nobody would pick.
- A picture question is on the chapter's subject, says what to look at, and can be answered from the picture.
- Think-first sits on easy questions. The explain follows a piece with a cause or a why. The first question is easy.
- Questions ask about what the text says, causes, order, people, places; not trivia.
- Nothing sets a clock, a race, a penalty or a streak.

**Words**
- The same to every child: no «έτοιμη/έτοιμος», no «ποια/ποιος» addressed to her, no gendered adjective about her.
- Singular, short sentences, the book's words. Nothing of the writer's own with brackets or abbreviations.
- The open questions in `talk` are the book's, in the singular.

Grade each finding: **blocking** (untrue, or she can't answer it), **should-fix**, **nit**. Say where (piece or
step number) and what would fix it. Nothing wrong is a fine answer; don't invent findings.
